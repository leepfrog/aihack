# Where It Went

An interactive spending visualizer. Import a CSV export from your bank or credit card and see how much money went where.

**Open `index.html` in a browser** — no install or build step. Keep `geo-data.js` next to it (it holds the map shapes and city coordinates). Your data never leaves your browser.

## What it shows

- **Summary**: total spent, monthly average, biggest category, largest purchase.
- **Treemap**: every box is sized by dollars spent, grouped by category. Click a category to zoom in on its merchants; click a merchant to filter everything to it.
- **Top merchants**: the ten places you spent the most.
- **Where you were**: a US map of where purchases happened. *Cities* shows a circle per city sized by spending; *States* shades each state. Click a place (on the map or in the list) to filter everything to it; picking a city in the list zooms to it. Zoom with the +/− buttons, pinch, or Ctrl+scroll. Online purchases and ones with no location are listed separately so nothing is hidden.
- **Month by month**: stacked columns by category with the monthly average. Click a month to focus on it.
- **Transactions**: searchable, sortable list. Change a merchant's category and all of its transactions move with it.

Filter by date range (presets or From/To months) and toggle categories on or off with the chips.

## Importing your data

1. Download a CSV of transactions from your bank or card site (most have "Download" or "Export" on the activity page).
2. Click **Import CSV** (or drag the file onto the page).
3. Check the column matching: Date, Description, Amount (or a Debit column), and optionally Category, City and State. Pick whether spending appears as negative or positive numbers — it's auto-detected.
4. Tick "Add to the data already loaded" to combine several accounts. Duplicate rows are skipped.

Refunds, income and card payments/transfers are left out. Categories are guessed from merchant names (or taken from your file's Category column); fix any in the table. Your imported data and category changes are saved in your browser's local storage. **Clear data** wipes them.

## Locations

Where a purchase happened comes from your file's City/State columns when it has them (Amex-style "City/State" works too). Otherwise it's read from the end of the description, where most banks print it, e.g. `STARBUCKS STORE 05521 SAN FRANCISCO CA`. City names cut short by the bank (`SAN FRANCISC CA`) are matched by prefix. Cities are matched against US places with 12,000+ people; smaller towns are counted at the state level.

Map data in `geo-data.js` is generated by `scripts/build-geo.js` from us-atlas (US Census Bureau boundaries) and GeoNames city data (CC BY 4.0).
